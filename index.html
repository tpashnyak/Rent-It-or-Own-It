Rent It or Own It
--:--

Personal Finance · 45-minute interactive lesson

Is renting really throwing money away?

Today you play the game show where every contestant gets a different life. You'll run the real numbers on renting and buying, test a duplex house hack, and survive a few plot twists before you make the call.

Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you will learn:

  • the trade-offs between renting (flexibility, lower upfront cost, fewer repair bills) and buying (stability, equity)
  • the full cost of owning a home, beyond the mortgage payment
  • how the length of time you plan to stay changes the math
  • why an emergency fund matters as much as a down payment
  • how house hacking works, and the risks that come with it

Segment 1 · 5 minutes

Warm Up: Myth or Fact?

Read each statement. Thumbs up for Fact, thumbs down for Myth, flat hand for It depends. Then reveal.

Teacher move

Ask one student from the "Myth" side and one from the "Fact" side to defend their vote before revealing. The point to land: the right answer usually depends on the person's situation.

Segment 2 · 7 minutes

The Showdown: Renter vs. Owner

Neither side wins automatically. Each one buys you something different.

Contestant 1

The Renter

  • Flexibility: move when the lease ends
  • Lower upfront cost: usually a deposit and first month's rent
  • Fewer repair bills: the landlord replaces the furnace
  • Predictable for now: but rent can rise at renewal
  • No equity: rent pays for a place to live, not ownership
vs.

Contestant 2

The Owner

  • Stability: no landlord can decide not to renew
  • Equity: each payment builds ownership
  • Fixed principal and interest: on a fixed-rate loan
  • Big upfront cost: down payment plus closing costs
  • Every repair is yours: roof, water heater, plumbing

The mortgage payment is only the opening bid

Being able to afford the payment does not mean you're ready to buy. Owners also pay:

Principal + InterestThe loan payment itself.
Property taxSet by your county; can rise after reassessment.
Homeowners insuranceRequired by the lender.
PMIPrivate mortgage insurance, often required with less than 20% down.
MaintenanceA common rule of thumb: budget about 1% of the home's price per year.
Closing & sellingFees when you buy (often 2–5%) and when you sell (often 5–6%).

Equity = what your home is worth − what you still owe on it.

Teacher move

Quick check: "Name one cost the renter never pays." Cold-call two groups. Then ask, "Why would someone choose renting even if they could afford to buy?" Listen for flexibility and keeping savings available.

Segment 3 · 12 minutes

Persona Lab: Run Their Numbers

Each group gets a life. Draw one, or let the teacher assign. Adjust the numbers, then decide what your persona should do and why.

Persona

Example assumptions: 30-year fixed loan, property tax 1% of price per year, insurance $150/month, maintenance 1% per year, PMI 0.6% of the loan per year when down payment is under 20%, closing costs 3%, selling costs 6%, home values and rent grow 3% per year.

Emergency fund check

After the down payment and closing costs, how many months of expenses could they cover?

Many planners suggest keeping 3 to 6 months of expenses saved.
Teacher move
  • Have each group change only Years they'll stay and watch the break-even point. This is the "aha" moment for most students.
  • Ask: "Your persona can afford the payment. Are they ready to buy? Check the emergency fund."
  • Groups record: persona, recommendation (rent or buy), and two numbers that justify it.

Flip the risk cards

Teacher move

Slide vacancy from 0 to 6 months. Ask: "At what point does the house hack stop helping?" Then flip the risk cards and discuss which risk would worry your persona most. Mention that some homeowners later keep a first home as a rental and add properties over time, which multiplies both the reward and the risk.

Deal a card. It hits the persona currently loaded in the Persona Lab (—). Huddle for 60 seconds: does the decision still hold?

Exit Ticket: Final Answer

Make the call

Personal finance is not about one answer that works for everyone. It's about knowing your options, running the numbers, and choosing what fits your goals.
